How it works

Every image goes through the same three steps.

1

File history

We first look inside the file for signs it was made by AI: Content Credentials (C2PA), EXIF and XMP fields, and PNG text. Only a claim of AI generation counts, because camera tags are easy to fake.

2

Five close-ups

If the file says nothing, we cut five 224-pixel squares from the centre and the four corners, at full resolution, and pass each one through a frozen DINOv2 vision model.

3

One score

A classifier scores each square and we average the five. At 0.98 or above the image is flagged; from 0.5 it's inconclusive; below that it isn't flagged.

How well it works

We trained on 300 Stable Diffusion images and 300 real photos, then tested on 300 images from Flux, a generator the model had never seen, and 300 more real photos.

Good to know

A score is a signal, not proof either way.

  • File history is a claim, not a measurement. Tags can be added to real photos and removed from AI ones.
  • Content Credentials are accepted unless they're marked invalid; we don't yet check who signed them.
  • In our Flux test, AI files were smaller on average than real photos (51 KB vs 70 KB), so that score may be slightly high.
  • A few real photos score like AI, which is why the flag line sits as high as 0.98. Most AI images won't be flagged.
  • "Not flagged" never proves an image is real. New AI tools can slip past any detector.
